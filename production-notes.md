1. In general, a number of things have changed from the Gutenberg text during proofreading. Their text comes from a different edition from the first-edition page scans. Among other things, their text uses British spelling and quotations, which have been changed to American style to match the page scans. 2. In Book 3, Chapter 4, two phrases are marked as French and italicized: noblesse oblige and amour propre. Although these appear in the Merriam Webster dictionary, a decision has been made to consider them foreign here, because they are sufficiently obscure and because the character speaking is meant to come across as learned and pretentious. They are italicized in the page scans. 3. In Book 1, Chapter 4, the text refers to “Fuchs’s story.” The page scans say “Jake’s story,” though later editions of the book have “Fuchs’s story.” It's likely that “Jake’s story” is an error in the first edition, since it is indeed Fuchs who has just told a story. An editorial decision has been made to retain “Fuchs’s story” from the e-text for this edition. 4. There seems to be some inconsistency with references to people's homes, such as the Cutters’ versus the Cutters. In general, the text has been kept to match the page scans, as one could read them as referring to, say, the Cutters’ house or the Cutters themselves. However, in Book 2, Chapter 12, there is a clear error in “the Cutter’s yard,” which has been changed here to “the Cutters’ yard.” In Book 4, Chapter 2, “the Harling’s Tony” has been changed to “the Harlings’ Tony.”
